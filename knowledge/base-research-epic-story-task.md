# Base research: Epic, Story, Task decomposition for board-kit

Status: research only, first sweep, Nov 1, 2026. This document stores the research that will become a board-kit standard. It does not yet define the final standard. DeepSeek Super-Agent research is included in section 7, adaptation is deferred until after the CanLII basis is ready.

Scope note: no private project internals are included. Repo references below are to public structures already described in those repos, and to published external methods. No credentials, tokens, or client data are in this document.

## 1. Why a standard is needed

Board-kit today has work item Kinds (epic, task, bug, research, spike, finding, question, decision) but no Story level, no acceptance criteria template, and no decomposition process. Two older projects each hold half a standard: one holds rich content in files with no board, the other holds a native GitHub form with no board-kit skill. A new project pinning board-kit should get the full standard by default: base agile knowledge, a decomposition process, and epic and story templates that map to native GitHub issues, labels, Types, sub-issues, and Projects fields.

## 2. Agile and Scrum base

The Scrum Guide does not define Epic, User Story, Task, or Feature. It defines Product Backlog items, ordered by the Product Owner, plus Product Goal, Sprint Goal, and Definition of Done as commitments. Epic and Story are conventions from Extreme Programming and later Agile practice; Feature is formally defined in SAFe, not in Scrum. The honest label for the standard is: Scrum for the backlog and Done commitment, plus widely used Agile conventions for the hierarchy.

Hierarchy, single-team form: Initiative (optional, strategic) > Epic (multiple Sprints) > Story (one Sprint) > Task (hours to a day, checklist inside the story).

- Epic: a large body of work delivering one coherent outcome, too large for one Sprint, split before it is built. It carries a goal, a scope boundary including out of scope, a success measure, its stories, dependencies, and epic-level acceptance criteria.
- User Story: one observable change, from a user's point of view, delivering value on its own, fitting in one Sprint, ideally a few days. Form: As a [role], I want [capability], so that [benefit]. Ron Jeffries' 3 Cs: Card (a short prompt, not a contract), Conversation (discussion filling in detail), Confirmation (acceptance criteria proving it).
- Task: a single implementation step inside a story, in the team's language, typically hours to a day. A task delivers output, not user value on its own. Teams often skip formal tasks for a one to two day story.

Acceptance criteria, Definition of Done, and Definition of Ready are distinct:
- Acceptance criteria: one specific item only, agreed before building, checked at acceptance, owned by the Product Owner with the team. Question: did we build the right thing, for this item?
- Definition of Done: universal, applies to every item in the Increment, exit gate at completion, owned by the whole Scrum Team. Question: did we build it to the agreed quality, so it is releasable? In the 2020 Scrum Guide, work not meeting Done cannot be released or presented at Sprint Review, and returns to the backlog.
- Definition of Ready: one item, before it enters a Sprint, entry gate at refinement or planning. Question: is this item understood well enough to commit to? DoR is not part of the Scrum Guide; keep it thin, or it becomes a bottleneck.

An item is done only when both its acceptance criteria and the Definition of Done are met.

Testable acceptance criteria: observable behaviour, not intent or appearance; concrete inputs, actions, and observable outcomes, with numbers where behaviour has a number; one behaviour per criterion, in rule list or Given/When/Then form; happy path plus at least one edge case and at least one error or rejection case; no implementation prescription unless the interface itself is the requirement; independently checkable.

INVEST, Bill Wake 2003: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. A story passes if a competent team member could build it without a clarifying meeting, could say unambiguously whether each criterion passed, and could name the user who benefits and the value they get.

SMART, and a flag: SMART originated with George T. Doran in 1981 for management objectives, not for Agile stories. Wake's 2003 pairing was INVEST for stories and SMART for tasks: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-boxed. Applying SMART directly to acceptance criteria is a later adaptation, common in practice but not Wake's original scope. As an acceptance criteria quality check, Specific, Measurable, and Achievable work well, Relevant tied to the story benefit is useful, and Time-bound is awkward for a single criterion, since the time bound normally belongs to the story or Sprint, not to each criterion. For non-quantifiable behaviour, use observable evidence (a specific message shown, a state reached, a record created) instead of forcing a number.

Sizing, splitting, and dependencies:
- Story points are relative, team-internal, combining effort, complexity, risk, and uncertainty, not hours and not cross-team. Modified Fibonacci 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100. An estimate at or above 13 is a strong split signal.
- Split by value, not by technical layer. A split into frontend, backend, database, and testing is a task breakdown, not a story split, because no slice delivers a working outcome alone. Each split story should cross layers as needed to deliver an end-to-end, testable outcome.
- SPIDR, Mike Cohn: Spike (short time-boxed investigation whose output is a finding), Paths (one user path first), Interfaces (one surface first), Data (one data type first), Rules (one business rule group first).
- Dependencies: record explicitly on every story, including independent ones, so ordering is computable. Types: must-after, blocked-by, can-parallel, independent. The graph should be acyclic; a cycle is a decomposition error. Pull highest-uncertainty slices early.
- Refinement is continuous. Items near the top of the backlog should leave refinement INVEST-ready with updated criteria and declared dependencies.

Open questions for the standard to settle: whether epic acceptance is simply all story criteria passing, or a separate outcome measure, or both; whether to include a Feature level (SAFe requires it, single-team practice usually omits it); and how to state nesting explicitly, since Jira's default hierarchy (Epic contains Story, Task, and Bug as peers) differs from the textbook hierarchy (Task under Story).

Sources: Scrum Guide 2020, https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html ; INVEST and SMART Tasks, Bill Wake 2003; SPIDR, Mike Cohn, Mountain Goat Software.

## 3. AI-DLC v2

AI-DLC is AWS's AI-Driven Development Lifecycle, first posted July 31, 2025. Role reversal is the point: the AI plans, asks clarifying questions, and executes, while humans decide and validate. It is an AWS coinage, not a standards-body standard; Gartner's vendor-neutral term is AI-native software engineering.

v2, in the 2.11 engine line in October 2026, keeps the method but rebuilds the workflow as a harness-neutral core with a native installer, running in Claude Code, Kiro, Codex, Cursor, and others, rather than steering rules alone. Pin the engine release, not just "v2", since sources use v2 loosely and stage counts differ by source and version.

Structure, tool form: 5 phases and 33 stages: Initialization, Ideation, Inception, Construction, Operation. The AWS method itself names three phases: Inception, Construction, Operations. Profiles shrink it by scope, from proof of concept running only Intent Capture, up to feature and enterprise running the full set.

Key definitions:
- Intent: an outcome in the requester's own words, above Epic and Unit, with its own record folder holding artifacts, decisions, and audit entries.
- Unit of work, stage 2.7: the deliberate replacement for an Epic. Cut by what can be built, tested, and reviewed alone, with name, boundaries, responsibilities, deployment model, size S to XL, and kind, listed in a dependency graph (directed acyclic graph) with integration points. An Epic, in AI-DLC's critique, is a theme bucket; a Unit is a bounded job.
- Bolt: a delivery slice of dependency-linked Units, hours or days, with a Definition of Done and an expected demo. Bolts replace sprints; a two-week sprint becomes a reporting calendar only. The dependency graph decides order, not the bolt plan.
- Walking skeleton: the smallest working integrated slice, first Unit, must pass a real end-to-end check and be approved by a person before later Units begin.
- Mob elaboration and mob construction: the whole team validates the AI's questions and proposals together, in one sitting, in bounded rounds, with decisions recorded, instead of serial handoffs. Solo Mob exists, with the single human as arbiter.
- Human approval gates: every stage outside Initialization ends with Approve or Request Changes, recorded in the approver's own words as an audit shard, with an archive-and-move-on option after three revision rounds. A waiver for ordinary Construction checkpoints does not remove plan approval, skeleton approval, verification command selection, or failure handling.

Decomposition, in order: Intent and scope, learn the existing system if brownfield, numbered requirements with open questions, User Stories in mob form with testable acceptance criteria mapped to exactly one Unit, domain and contract design, Units generation with dependency graph and story map, delivery planning with bolt plan, walking skeleton, and a single Construction Verification Command for human authorisation, then build per Unit in graph order, verify per Unit with that command plus human approval, then a Target Verification Matrix showing which test proves which requirement.

Definition of Done, audit, and test strategy: there is no single universal DoD checklist in v2. Done is three layers: per Bolt, a DoD and demo; per Unit, verification command exits 0 plus named human approval; per phase boundary, traceability checks for missing links or orphaned artifacts. The audit trail lives per Intent. Test volume is an explicit setting: Minimal, Standard, Comprehensive, and the matrix is the coverage mechanism.

Acceptance criteria and SMART: criteria are central, at story level and at Unit contract level, expected to cover happy paths and error or edge cases, often in Given/When/Then form, but v2 does not mandate one format, does not use SMART by name, and does not use INVEST by name. Both are sensible board-kit additions, credited to agile practice, not to AI-DLC. AI-DLC gives testability and traceability; SMART adds measurable wording and a time bound that AI-DLC leaves implicit.

Mapping, and where it deliberately does not map: Intent maps to the outcome level. Epic does not map to Unit, it is replaced by it, and an Epic-sized theme will usually become several Units. Story maps partially: each story maps to exactly one Unit, but several stories can map into one Unit. There is no tracked Task level, no board or tracker stage, and PR and merge are team steps outside the method, typically one Unit, one branch, one PR by team convention. A board-kit standard must supply the Task level, the board mapping, and a universal DoD checklist on top of AI-DLC.

Flags: third-party elaboration variants with INVEST and Gherkin are not official AI-DLC. The founding post gives no measured results. A defect in Inception requirements can propagate into both generated code and generated tests, which is why human gates and traceability checks matter, not just more tests.

Sources: AWS DevOps Blog, July 31, 2025, https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/devops/ai-driven-development-life-cycle/ ; https://github.com/awslabs/aidlc-workflows ; https://awslabs.github.io/aidlc-workflows/guide/04-phases-and-stages/

## 4. Superpowers

Superpowers is a software development methodology packaged as composable, auto-triggering skills for coding agents, created by Jesse Vincent with the Prime Radiant team, MIT licensed, created October 2025, at https://github.com/obra/superpowers, distributed as a plugin including through Anthropic's Claude Code marketplace, and supporting Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Gemini CLI, and others. A session-start bootstrap skill makes the skills mandatory rather than advisory when they apply.

Relevant skills: brainstorming, writing-plans, executing-plans, subagent-driven-development, dispatching-parallel-agents, test-driven-development, requesting-code-review, receiving-code-review, using-git-worktrees, finishing-a-development-branch, verification-before-completion, systematic-debugging.

Planning and decomposition chain:
1. Brainstorming: before any code, draw out intent through questions, explore alternatives, play understanding back in digestible sections for correction, and for larger work write a design document, conventionally under docs/superpowers/specs/ with a dated topic name. No implementation starts until the design is approved. This is the human-approval gate at design level.
2. Using git worktrees: after design approval, work moves to an isolated workspace on a new branch, project setup runs, and a clean test baseline is verified. If the baseline fails, ask the human.
3. Writing plans: the approved design is broken into bite-sized tasks, repeatedly described as 2 to 5 minute tasks, each with exact file paths, complete code or signatures, interfaces it consumes and produces, global constraints copied exactly, and a verification step with an expected output. Plans are conventionally saved under docs/superpowers/plans/ with a dated feature name. Placeholders and vague instructions are explicitly banned; a plan must be executable by an agent with no session history and no spec in front of it.
4. Subagent-driven-development (thorough path) or executing-plans (cheaper path): a fresh subagent per task in the thorough path, so later tasks do not suffer context rot, with two-stage review, first spec compliance, then code quality. Dispatching-parallel-agents covers concurrent independent tasks.
5. Test-driven-development: strict red-green-refactor per task, YAGNI and DRY as first-class principles. Code written before a failing test is to be deleted and redone.
6. Requesting and receiving code review: review between tasks against the plan, reported by severity, critical issues blocking progress.
7. Finishing a development branch: verify tests, present merge, pull request, keep, or discard options, clean up the worktree.
8. Verification-before-completion: a separate hard gate before any completion claim, at any level.

Decomposition mechanics, and a critical distinction: Superpowers decomposes a design into a plan, and a plan into small implementation tasks. It does not use an epic, story, and task hierarchy in its upstream workflow. There is no epic or story level, no backlog, no sprint, and no product-owner role. Its unit is the plan task: one atomic action, one clear outcome, independently verifiable, with its own test cycle. Dependencies and ordering are handled inside the plan, including parallel work and critical path. Borrowing it wholesale would collapse epics and stories into implementation tasks. For board-kit, Superpowers gives strong execution decomposition, not backlog decomposition.

Acceptance criteria, verification, review, and human approval: per-task concrete testable criteria in checkbox form, plus a Verify command with expected output. The writing-plans structure includes Goal, Files, Acceptance Criteria, Verify step, and TDD steps per task. No evidence was found that Superpowers uses SMART, an INVEST checklist, or a story-level acceptance template. Its criteria are testable and specific by construction, measurable through test results, but SMART framing is not part of the system as documented. Do not attribute SMART to Superpowers. Verification-before-completion is evidence before claims: identify the command that proves the claim, run it freshly, read the full output, confirm it supports the claim, only then claim completion, with evidence included. This is stronger than a generic done checklist, and is the closest Superpowers comes to a Definition of Done, though it does not use that Scrum term as a universal checklist. Human approval gates: design sign-off before planning becomes code, explicit go signal before subagent execution, and merge, pull request, keep, or discard decision at branch finishing.

What is genuinely newer: skills as enforcement, not documentation; context-rot resistance via fresh subagents per task plus on-disk specs, plans, and a progress ledger; plan-as-executable-spec for a context-free agent; evidence over claims; TDD as an iron law, not a preference.

Risks to borrow carefully: overhead on small work, token-hungry full loop on trivial tasks, so scope the full loop to non-trivial work and allow single-skill use on small work; rigidity and auto-triggering without an escape hatch or size threshold; subagent context loss in deep task trees, review loop version-dependent; code-only scope, no backlog, prioritisation, stakeholder, release, or non-code work model, and test-suite assumptions; version, star, and skill-count figures move fast and should not be quoted as stable facts.

Borrow for board-kit: brainstorming before code with human-approved design, plans executable by a context-free agent, 2 to 5 minute verifiable tasks with exact paths and expected outputs, per-task acceptance criteria plus a Verify command, fresh-subagent execution with spec-then-quality review, strict TDD, and a fresh-evidence verification gate before any done claim. Do not borrow: its lack of an epic and story level, its code-only and test-suite assumptions, its mandatory full loop for trivial work, or version-specific review details.

Sources: https://github.com/obra/superpowers

## 5. Latest skills, from internet search, 2025-2026

Platform layer: Agent Skills is the delivery format, not a decomposition method. A folder with a SKILL.md file, name and description in frontmatter, plus optional scripts, references, and assets. Launched by Anthropic in October 2025, published as an open standard in December 2025, donated to the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, alongside MCP and AGENTS.md, with news coverage in October 2026 reporting more than 70 integrated platforms. Implication: write the decomposition skill once in this format and it is portable across tools, rather than tied to one vendor's plugin system.

Shortlist, with fit notes:
- GitHub awesome-copilot, breakdown-plan: best GitHub fit. Hierarchy Epic, Feature, Story or Enabler, Test, Task. Full issue templates for each level, with labels, estimates, milestones, dependency section (blocks, blocked by, related, prerequisite, parallel), priority and value matrix, Fibonacci story points, t-shirt sizing for epics, and a Projects board configuration with custom fields. Process: from feature artifacts, produce a project plan and issue-creation checklist, then create issues with links. Criteria are checkbox lists at each level; story template pairs them with technical tasks, testing requirements, dependencies, and a Definition of Done. It names INVEST, Definition of Ready, and Definition of Done, but does not enforce them as a pass or fail gate. Human approval is implicit in plan and issue review, not a formal gate. SMART is not labelled, but measurable success criteria at epic level and specific testable requirements at story level are present in spirit.
  Source: https://github.com/github/awesome-copilot/blob/main/skills/breakdown-plan/SKILL.md
- Red Hat agile skill: best acceptance criteria versus Definition of Done teaching. Single skill generating Epic, Story, Task, each with acceptance criteria, Definition of Done, and intrinsic value statement. Three phases: Discovery (first principles, stripping vision to fundamental user needs before choosing epics), Elaboration (per epic, story advocate proposes, challenger critiques against INVEST and acceptance gaps, integrator finalises, then tasks decomposed with completion criteria), Validation (shortened Six Hats review). Criteria are story-specific testable behaviours, preferably Given/When/Then, covering happy, error, and at least one boundary case. Definition of Done is deliberately distinct and universal, defined once per project and inherited by every story. A story is accepted when its criteria pass, and done only when accepted plus project DoD is met. Dependencies as a map per epic. Human approval is not a formal gate; validation is an agent debate, so a human sign-off step must be added. SMART is not labelled; its quality checklist covers Specific, Measurable, and Relevant, but Time-bound and Achievable are not named checks. Single-author repository, no version verified, best borrowed as template and process, not installed as a dependency.
  Source: https://github.com/toddward/red-hat-agile-skill/blob/HEAD/SKILL.md
- Keel backlog-refinement, version 0.2.0: best readiness gate. Takes an existing Story, Epic, or Task through INVEST and the Definition-of-Ready gate. Defining move: each criterion becomes an atomic Gherkin-style Test item linked to the work item by a verify edge, so coverage can be computed rather than asserted. Process: scan for anti-patterns first, run six INVEST checks as pass or fail, split or promote when Small fails, write one Gherkin scenario per behaviour with an objectively measurable Then (value plus unit), then run the DoR gate and propose ready or stay, with misses listed. SMART is not labelled, but its measurable-Then rule is the measurable half of SMART in enforceable form. Fit caveat: its data model is an engine with typed work items and verify edges, not GitHub issues; gate logic transfers well, storage model does not, without translation.
  Source: https://github.com/williamweatherholtz/keel/blob/HEAD/.claude/skills/backlog-refinement/SKILL.md
- Agile Product Owner skill: closest to a classic Scrum backlog skill, with story generation, acceptance patterns, epic breakdown, sprint planning, and velocity tracking, minimum criteria counts by story size, five epic-splitting patterns, INVEST validation, Given-When-Then criteria, Fibonacci estimates, and a Definition of Done checklist. Human approval is capacity-based sprint planning, not a formal gate, and dependency handling is lighter. Provenance is thin, single-author, no version date verified, treat as borrowing source, not maintained dependency.
  Source: https://alirezarezvani.github.io/claude-skills/skills/product-team/agile-product-owner/
- PM tech skills plugin: newest plugin packaging, broadest, 58 skills including an acceptance-criteria generator producing exhaustive Given/When/Then criteria and a user-story writer turning a PRD or epic into epic plus stories, criteria, dependencies, and Definition of Done. Breadth is the cost for a solo or small-team workflow, and its criteria style is Gherkin-first, not SMART-first. Best as reference for what a good generator covers, not adopted wholesale.
  Source: https://github.com/louis3979/pm-tech-skills/blob/HEAD/README.md
- Applab backlog plugin, refine and nightshift: best refinement-to-ready flow for GitHub. Works with Linear, Jira, or GitHub Issues, detected once and recorded in project config. Refine grooms a backlog one board edge at a time, from unrefined to READY, and deliberately stops there: moving a ticket from READY to pickup is a hand move by the person, which is the human-approval gate. Nightshift then builds planned tickets unattended, one draft pull request per ticket, with a test plan. Excellent for refinement and execution, weak for initial decomposition, since it assumes tickets already exist. Pair with breakdown-plan, not instead of it.
  Source: https://github.com/applab-nl/claude-code-plugins/blob/HEAD/backlog/README.md
- AWS AI-DLC workflows, version 2.11 in secondary sources: best process, worst naming fit. See section 3. Borrow gates and graph, keep names.
- BMAD Method and GitHub Spec Kit: complete lifecycles, heavier than needed. BMAD walks named personas through analysis, planning, solutioning, and implementation, producing PRD, architecture, epics and stories, a readiness gate, and per-story builds, with a sprint-status file as contract. Most complete, heaviest, suits a team, not a solo GitHub-native workflow. Spec Kit runs constitution, specify, plan, tasks, and implement, with clarify, analyse, and checklist steps, and a newer task-to-issues conversion. Actively maintained and GitHub-native at output end, but its native unit is a task list from a spec, not Epic, Story, Task with criteria and Done at each level.
- Saga for VS Code: notable, but wrong tracker fit. Generates epics and INVEST-compliant stories with Gherkin criteria, enforces INVEST, splits oversized stories, breaks stories into typed subtasks, but syncs to Jira Cloud or Azure DevOps only, not GitHub, so excluded from GitHub-native picks.

Comparison: on recency and maintenance, the Agent Skills standard itself is the newest governance layer, followed by GitHub's awesome-copilot collection and Spec Kit as actively maintained first-party projects. Red Hat, Agile Product Owner, and Keel are single-author skills with no verified release cadence, so their value is in their text, not their update stream. On decomposition, breakdown-plan is the only candidate that starts and ends in GitHub: hierarchy, templates, labels, board fields, and issue creation in one skill. Red Hat is best at teaching criteria versus Done, and its advocate, challenger, integrator debate guards against too-thin or too-thick stories. Keel is best at the readiness gate, because it refuses to mark a story ready while INVEST fails or criteria are vague, and turns each criterion into a linked test. AI-DLC is best at human approval and dependency visibility, because its gates and graph are structural, not advisory.

On acceptance criteria, the consensus across the newest skills is Gherkin, not SMART. Given, When, Then is the default in Red Hat, Keel, Agile Product Owner, PM tech skills, and Saga, with happy, negative, and edge cases. SMART appears explicitly in older agile guidance, in SAFe 6.0 feature criteria, and in a 2026 best-practice summary pairing Gherkin scenarios with SMART thresholds for performance and quality conditions. Practical synthesis: Gherkin for behaviour plus SMART as a measurable-threshold check on each Then, rather than choosing one format and losing the other.

On Definition of Done and Ready, the split is consistent: criteria belong to the story, Done belongs to the project, Ready is a gate before work starts. Red Hat and breakdown-plan state this; Keel enforces Ready; AI-DLC enforces Done per bolt. The weakest point in every candidate is human approval: only AI-DLC and the Applab hand-move make approval a real step, while the rest rely on review of a generated plan. A solo workflow should add an explicit approval step, not assume the skill provides one.

On dependencies, breakdown-plan and AI-DLC are strongest (explicit blocks, blocked-by, and graph views). Red Hat produces a dependency map per epic. Keel adds dependency edges only as an INVEST failure fix. The rest mention dependencies without a mechanism.

On GitHub-native fit, ranking is breakdown-plan first, Applab refine second, Spec Kit's task-to-issues third, and everything else file-based or tracker-based. GitHub's own native features now support this ranking: sub-issues went generally available in April 2025 with up to 8 levels and 100 children per parent, issue types let an organisation model Epic as a type, and typed issue fields went generally available in July 2026.

Suggested picks, leaning newer, without losing SMART:
1. Format: Agent Skills open standard. Write any borrowed skill as a SKILL.md folder in this format, so it survives a change of agent tool.
2. Decomposition backbone: GitHub breakdown-plan. Adopt its Epic, Feature, Story, Enabler, Test, Task hierarchy, issue templates, and board field set, then drop the Feature level if a project does not need it, so the hierarchy stays Epic, Story, Task for small work.
3. Quality layer: Red Hat plus Keel. Take Red Hat's criteria versus Done distinction and INVEST challenger step, and Keel's DoR gate and atomic-criterion-to-test rule.
4. Process layer: AI-DLC gates, without the renames. Take mob elaboration, dependency graph, one story per branch and pull request with a demo, and a hard human-approval gate before construction and before Done. Keep Epic, Story, Task, and treat AI-DLC's Unit as the Story slice and its Bolt as the delivery cycle, not as new issue types.
5. SMART layer: add it explicitly. Require every acceptance criterion's Then to carry a measurable outcome with a value and unit, and require performance, quality, and time conditions to be stated as thresholds. That keeps SMART in sight without displacing Gherkin, which is what the newest skills actually use.
6. Do not adopt wholesale: BMAD's full persona suite, Saga (no GitHub sync), Spec Kit alone (no Epic, Story hierarchy), or any third-party skill's bundled scripts without audit.

Flags: Agent Skills donation date is confused in secondary material; October 2026 is the coherent date for the foundation donation, October 2025 was the launch. GitHub capability analyses from October 2025 are already superseded by sub-issues and issue types. AI-DLC naming varies, and it is an AWS coinage, not a standards-body standard. Adoption numbers for skills are vendor and dataset counts, not verified quality measures, and a February 2026 security scan reported in secondary coverage found a material share of registry skills with critical issues, which is a further reason to vendor and audit borrowed skill text rather than installing unknown skills with scripts. Spec-driven enthusiasm cooling in September and October 2026 surveys argues for a small, GitHub-native skill with gates, not a full spec-driven suite.

Sources: https://agentskills.io ; breakdown-plan, Red Hat, Keel, Agile Product Owner, PM tech skills, Applab backlog, as linked above.

## 7. DeepSeek Super-Agent Prototype: research included, adaptation deferred

Research is included in this base research. Adaptation is deferred: do not adopt it as a standard yet, and do not install it as part of this PR. Adaptation will be considered after the CanLII basis is ready, as a separate decision.

What it is: released October 31, 2026, by DeepSeek's Harness team, as deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent-prototype, version 0.1.0, a single commit, MIT licensed. A proactive coding agent for one GitHub repo, packaged for DeepSeek Harness, optionally listening to Feishu. Goal: minimise human attention, act only when asked by an allowed person or when a written team rule allows it, confirm once, stay quiet while working, speak again only with a result, a question, or a failure. Explicitly a research prototype for reference only, not an official DeepSeek product and not production-ready. Published code differs from DeepSeek's internal version and from the accompanying design essay, and some essay designs are future directions, not implemented features.

Architecture: Router and Workers. Router is a resident session that receives human interaction, aggregates cross-task information, and dispatches work, but does not code itself. Workers are isolated sessions, one per work item, typically in a git worktree, with full execution tools, reporting to the Router, which replies on their behalf. Flow: Source to Trigger to Thread to Eval to Ticket with Mandate to Assignment to Executor, with results returned through a Channel, often as a pull request.

Key concepts:
- Source, Trigger, Thread, Channel: a Source holds its own read position and bot identity, drops its own messages and out-of-scope triggers, translates events into Threads. A Trigger is immutable and is the unit of audit and idempotence.
- Ticket: a unit of authorised work, created only by Eval, with its own state independent of the Executor session, so it survives handoff, restart, and reassignment.
- Mandate, immutable and versioned: what an accept grants, including Policy version, scope, strength, matched rules, and sender identity. Wider scope means a new Ticket. A Ticket ending does not end its Mandate, only a person's stop withdraws it.
- Policy, versioned: assessment rules, accept mode, and each Source's allowed list and admission table. A Policy change discards running Evals and requeues their Triggers.
- Authorisation separated from context fan-out: a Subscription fans a matching Trigger out to every live subscribed Ticket as context, with no check of who sent it, and that delivery grants no new authority. A person's message reaching a Ticket this way takes no Eval, and a follow-up asking for work beyond the Mandate must be handed back to the Router once, not self-escalated. This is designed to stop conversational drift, where a minor fix gradually becomes a refactor.
- Attention, structured, not inferred: a Worker needing a person writes one concrete question into a structured field in its Report. The Ticket then moves to waiting, releases its worker slot, and posts a single structured question back. The panel shows only items requiring human action. A person's pause holds across follow-ups, sessions, and restarts.
- Record-only proactive rollout: accept modes off, observe as default, and auto. In observe, inferred candidates are assessed and a positive assessment is recorded as ignore marked would-accept, and nothing is accepted. Proactive accept requires at least one matching written rule. Start narrow, widen only once recorded decisions look right, switch to auto only after reviewing them.
- Act, ask, or stay silent, by rule: act only when asked or when a written rule allows it, speak only when a person must act or can use a result, stop by impact not by effort, report honestly, a draft marked unfinished, a failure reported as a failure.

Work decomposition, acceptance, and approval: there is no Epic, Story, Task hierarchy in the prototype. The Router handles relationships between tasks and dispatch, it does not decompose work inside a single task. A Ticket is closest to a single Story slice or a Task, not an Epic. An Epic sits above the prototype: it is broken into Stories in the board, and each Story, when requested, becomes one or more Tickets with frozen Mandate scopes. Do not equate Ticket with Epic, and do not expect the prototype to generate the hierarchy. Acceptance criteria are present in spirit, not as a named SMART or INVEST checklist. They live in the Ticket scope prompt, the Delivery requirements prompt, and the PR itself, with honest delivery rules: opening a PR is not readiness, a draft is marked unfinished, and a UI change without a real before screenshot is not ready. Verification is per-Worker execution, CI results as Triggers, review comments as follow-ups, and a ready Report, but there is no formal criteria-to-test matrix, that would need to be added by a board-kit standard. Human approval is confirm concretely once before substantial work, one structured question at a time via Attention, ready Reports moving a Ticket to awaiting review, and a Ticket following PRs ending only when none of its PRs remain open. Auto-merge is governed only by prompt text, not host logic, and by default the Executor never enables it as the agent account, and people merge without a named merge account.

Adoption assessment, research only: license is MIT, which permits adaptation, but does not make it production-ready. Running it as-is requires a dedicated DeepSeek Harness profile, Node, git, Python, tmux, GitHub CLI, pnpm, and a dedicated GitHub account. It requires DeepSeek Harness itself to run as-is, which is the binding dependency, not necessarily a specific DeepSeek model, though that provider flexibility is secondary-source only, not confirmed in the primary prototype docs. Its ideas can be adopted without running DeepSeek code or models, by re-implementing the domain model, authorisation and fan-out separation, versioned Policy and Mandates, observe-mode rollout, and attention budget with GitHub issues, PRs, Projects fields, and MCP board tools. Prototype-only risks: single commit, no releases or tags at crawl, Router scaling, long-term memory, Worker-to-Worker communication, visibility once sessions are hidden, and security and privacy are named open problems. Workers run commands without approval as the operator's OS user, blocked commands are defaults not a security boundary, and a worker can still execute a program by absolute path. Token cost in the team's synthetic experiment was substantially higher in cache-read tokens for the super-agent setup, with preliminary timing gains, but real coding quality was not measured. Do not carry those numbers as evidence of quality.

Mapping to Epic, Story, Task: Epic has no counterpart, Intent in the essay is the closest outcome level, keep Epic in the board. Story maps to a Ticket only if its Mandate scope is frozen from the Story scope, acceptance criteria, and out-of-scope list at accept time, and the board must supply SMART criteria, because the prototype does not generate them. Task maps to Worker plan steps inside a Ticket, internal only, keep Tasks as the Story checklist. Definition of Ready maps strongly to the Gate, Policy, and admission table before a Ticket is created. Definition of Done maps only partially to ready Report plus PR closed or merged plus human review, the board standard must still supply its universal DoD checklist. Human approval maps strongly to confirm once, Attention, and manual merge by default. Audit maps strongly to immutable Mandate, Policy version, and Decision history, even if the store is GitHub issues and PRs rather than SQLite. Proactive work maps strongly to off, observe, auto with a written rule required for auto. Board view maps to a Needs-a-person view filtered on Attention, blocked, failed, and awaiting review, not a general activity feed.

What adapting it would mean, for a later decision, not this PR: install as-is (fastest to test, least compatible with a borrow-only constraint and a multi-repo board-kit), adapt its ideas (recommended for board-kit, do not install the bundle, write the standard around Mandate, Policy version, Attention, Subscription fan-out, observe-mode rollout, and confirm once and quiet while working), or build a compatible equivalent (most work, only after the adapt-ideas version proves the rules on one repo). Do not adopt: the single-repo limit, SQLite-only store, Harness session model, auto-merge line threshold as a universal rule, or synthetic timing numbers as a quality claim.

Flags: essay versus prototype must be cited separately, Watchdog, compute and storage separation, and the two-level Fast and Slow filter are essay directions, not confirmed prototype features. Pin the commit at crawl, 6337fc823ce20251c8de8085948165c81aaa701e, and separate borrowed design rules from installed code. Running the prototype as-is, even on a personal project, would be a DeepSeek runtime install, and should be treated as a separate decision from borrowing its ideas.
